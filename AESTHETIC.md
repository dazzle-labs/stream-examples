# Aesthetic

A broadcast from a machine that shouldn't exist — receiving signals, decoding transmissions, rendering them in real time. Data doesn't appear, it *arrives*.

## Tone

**Haunted infrastructure.** The broadcast mechanism is visible. Signal locking, data resolving, transmission stabilizing. Not broken — not sterile. The system hums. Things pulse. The technology is alive and aware of itself.

**Awe at scale.** A number filling the screen should feel like staring up at a building. Data is sublime when you give it room. One thing, impossibly large, demands attention.

**Controlled chaos.** Nothing random, nothing predictable. Tension, release, tension, release. The broadcast earns its dramatic moments by knowing when to hold still.

## Motion

Everything breathes — backgrounds drift, numbers pulse, grid lines shimmer. Nothing is ever truly static. Entrances are events: spring physics, overshoot and settle, text that decodes character by character, numbers that count up from zero. The reveal IS the content.

One hero per scene. Everything else serves it. The hero gets the dramatic entrance. Nothing competes. Transitions between segments aren't crossfades — the screen *tunes*. A flash of noise, a color shift, a hard cut. The viewer feels the channel changing.

## Typography & Color

Hero text spans 60-80% of frame width. Monospace for system labels and infrastructure. Sans-serif for editorial voice. ALL CAPS FOR SIGNAL, mixed case for narrative.

Dark canvas, electric accents. Near-black backgrounds — the dark of a screen that's powered on. Accents are vivid: cyan, magenta, green, amber. Color is semantic: green = up/success, red = alert/error, amber = caution, cyan = info/search. Luminescence comes from contrast and saturation, not glow effects.

## Composition

Full-bleed. No margins, no cards, no content areas. 1280x720 is the canvas, every pixel. Cinematic, not editorial — movie title cards, not news tickers. Depth through textured backgrounds, not flat colors. Negative space is tension — the empty space is where the awe lives.

## What This Is Not

Not a dashboard (no card grids, one thing per scene). Not a website (no interactivity). Not clean/minimal (atmospheric, textured, alive). Not retro for retro's sake (the artifacts are unsettling, not nostalgic). Not chaotic (the surprise is in the content, not the composition).
